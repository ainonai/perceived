## Inheritance and environment

Inherited dispositions help shape both a person and the environments that person encounters. Upbringing, books at home, friendships, and a recommendation feed are not simply influences arriving from outside: interests and behavior help determine which experiences become available and which receive attention. Self-knowledge makes those patterns useful. Recognize a predisposition, follow how it compounds through experience, and decide which conditions to change or seek out. A genetic predisposition changes probabilities; it does not make every response inevitable. ### Evolution without a planner

Dawkins’s **survival machine** is an organism built by inherited information that persists through survival and reproduction. **Replication** connects appetite, attraction, protection, and other motives with what has continued across generations. You do not have to consciously want children for a reproductive reward system to influence what you want now. [V004 · 01:29](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=dovqFBFGOY4&t=89s) [V004 · 02:45](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=dovqFBFGOY4&t=165s) [V172 · 06:32](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=qXpniY_SjVw&t=392s)

Evolution has no conscious plan. Preparing for a date, choosing clothes, receiving a message, and enjoying intimacy can feel rewarding without anyone deliberately planning a child. The immediate experience is pleasure or anticipation; the evolutionary explanation concerns why dispositions producing those experiences persisted. **Environmental mismatch** occurs when an inherited inclination meets changed conditions. A tendency to store energy can become difficult to manage when snacks are always within reach. Changing what you keep at home works on the conditions producing the temptation, instead of leaving every late-night decision to willpower. ### Heritability is a population measure

Identical twins raised apart offer a way to examine inherited similarities across different homes. Imagine a clone growing up elsewhere: which interests and habits would still emerge? Robert Plomin’s *Blueprint*, a major influence on Julius, places genetic influence at the center of that question, including the tendency to seek experiences that fit existing dispositions. [V020 · 01:19](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Oz7CwcUmzDM&t=79s) [V199 · 21:46](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=VKc6PQpxjAU&t=1306s)

**Heritability** concerns differences within a population under particular conditions. A generation’s shared baseline and the ranking of people within it are different questions. Better nutrition and technology can raise a baseline without eliminating inherited differences among peers. This is the distinction behind Julius’s use of the **Flynn effect**, the rise in measured intelligence across generations. [V199 · 31:39](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=VKc6PQpxjAU&t=1899s)

**Genetic recombination** helps explain why siblings differ. Having a very tall brother does not mean you inherited the same combination of variants or were destined to reach his height. Development also depends on conditions: starvation, serious injury, and isolation are far removed from ordinary differences between homes. ### Dispositions help shape experience

**Heritability snowballing** is the compounding of predisposition and experience. An interest draws your attention; attention becomes practice; practice creates further opportunities in the same direction. To understand an environment’s influence, ask how someone came to spend time there in the first place. [V147 · 07:30](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=eZHlruluYDI&t=450s) [V201 · 03:49](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Vh7KSCqtCaI&t=229s)

Consider a book-filled home. Parents who enjoy reading supply books and can also pass on dispositions associated with reading. Julius’s technically gifted uncle offers another example: without ready access to the internet or a library, he took electronics apart and rebuilt them. An existing interest helped create its own opportunities for experience. Learning about a predisposition can change which foods you keep at home, which activities receive your attention, and which people become companions. Even a video about genetics is an environmental input. An existing interest can bring you to the video, and the knowledge gained can then change what you do. ### Individuals and populations

Cooperation and cheating also change the environments people encounter. Imagine two islands with different mixtures of cooperative and exploitative behavior. A willingness to cooperate can sustain reciprocal exchange where others return it; the same willingness produces different results where cheating dominates. The starting mixture changes the interactions that follow. [V147 · 25:37](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=eZHlruluYDI&t=1537s) [V147 · 26:26](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=eZHlruluYDI&t=1586s)

**Tit-for-tat** begins with cooperation, returns cooperation, and responds when the other person cheats. Give a newcomer the benefit of the doubt instead of creating an enemy before any exchange has occurred. Moving from these behavioral examples to genetic explanations of population differences requires a further hypothesis, examined in S12. Partner choice raises a further question: could different degrees of choice, sustained across generations, favor different combinations of physical and behavioral traits? Julius explores that possibility through national comparisons. He also questions whether his ChatGPT-generated tables are reliable and whether a flattering conclusion merely reflects what the audience wants to believe. ### Biotechnology and prediction

Embryo screening raises the prospect of selection beyond single-gene conditions toward probabilities for many traits. Unequal access would matter: advantages already available to wealthy parents could grow as reproductive technology and prediction improve. Julius’s 2023 forecast asks how far that difference could extend. [V029 · 01:18](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=I-lgwbsZrJc&t=78s) [V029 · 02:05](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=I-lgwbsZrJc&t=125s)

The number of embryos available for comparison changes the scale of that possibility. Choosing among five or six is very different from comparing an enormous number on a dashboard. Stem-cell research and AI-assisted selection enter Julius’s forecast as possible ways to expand the choice; the dashboard is an imagined future capability. Longevity poses a related question about biological limits. Treating one age-related disease leaves others waiting; slowing aging itself would address a common source of vulnerability.
