## 2. Sexual selection, replication, and why appearance matters to him

Dawkins’s “survival machine” is a body that preserves and reproduces inherited information. A preference can persist because it helps descendants reproduce: attractive sons provide the recurring human example. The peacock illustrates a feedback process in which preference favors an ornament even without a survival benefit. Marriage and economic obligations change the conditions in which preferences operate. Julius develops this replication argument alongside inserted biological explanations. ## 4. SMV is relational and perceived

**Sexual market value**, or SMV, is desirability relative to other people and available alternatives. A very attractive man dating an equally attractive woman can have less leverage than a less attractive man whose partner sees him as exceptional. That perceived discrepancy, rather than an absolute score of eight, supplies the comparison. Genuine relationships do not require everyone to clear one universal numerical cutoff. ## 7. Short-term sex, long-term partners, and the dual-mating hypothesis

Julius’s dual-mating account proposes that women can benefit reproductively from a stable provider and an affair with a man perceived to have better genes. In ancestral hypotheticals, deception avoids losing support or facing violence; in modern environments, lower consequences may make the strategy easier. He describes variation on a spectrum rather than saying every woman follows one strategy consciously. Some women seek both components in one man, and he repeatedly endorses an alpha/beta blend as the workable long-term arrangement.
