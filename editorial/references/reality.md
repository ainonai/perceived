# Principles of reality: biology, causation, mind, time, and cosmology

## 1. The common explanatory ambition

Julius tries to move beneath socially acceptable explanations to underlying processes. Attractive people are favored despite professions of fairness; self-interest operates beneath moral language; genetic dispositions help produce an apparently external environment; conscious choice sits within prior physical causes. In physics, familiar intuitions about a universal present and time’s flow likewise conceal a less intuitive structure. He repeatedly invites the listener to tolerate an uncomfortable explanation rather than protect a preferred story. ## 2. Evolution without conscious intention

Replicating information is the basic unit of his account of life. Organisms are temporary vehicles; inherited dispositions persist when their consequences helped replication. Genes do not plan, and evolution does not consciously foresee an outcome. In later purpose discussions he explicitly emphasizes that a strategy exists because it happened to work, not because genes reasoned toward it. Enjoyment and pain motivate behavior along paths he interprets as historically useful for reproduction, survival, care, and community continuation. Robert Plomin’s *Blueprint* is the most important recurring scientific influence. Julius uses twin comparisons, polygenic scores, correlations between home characteristics and achievement, and the increasing heritability of some traits with age to argue that inherited differences explain much more than people admit. He extends the argument to intelligence, personality, body weight, ambition, aggression, careers, political dispositions, and relationship behavior. ## 4. “Heritability snowballing” and the environment people help create

Julius’s “snowball” is a feedback account. Predispositions influence what people notice, enjoy, elicit from others, and pursue. Those environments supply additional practice and opportunities, so people become more like what he calls their true biological selves. A child interested in electronics finds material to dismantle; a curious person seeks books; a risk taker gravitates to particular projects. Parents’ books are not merely objects acting on a child: parents choose them, and pass dispositions to their children. ## 6. Determinism, randomness, and free will

Julius rejects an uncaused choosing self. If a decision follows from brain states, prior experiences, inherited dispositions, and physical law, he asks where an independent author could enter. If quantum randomness enters the chain, randomness is not a choice either. In 2022 he argues that taking this seriously should reduce hatred and pride: the homeless person did not choose all the causes of the situation, and the successful person did not earn the initial equipment. ## 10. The Big Bang and “before”

The 2022 origin video distinguishes an expanding universe from an ordinary explosion into pre-existing space. It discusses the historical contest with steady-state cosmology, the microwave background, and light-element abundance. It also says the Big Bang model does not explain the ultimate cause of time, energy, and space. Speculation then includes vacuum fluctuations, the Casimir effect, a Schwinger-effect analogy, baby universes, laboratory creation, and cyclic or multiple universes. He repeatedly labels the origin account speculative and says the answer requires a predictive account of quantum gravity. ## 11. Relativity and the present

Julius uses an Earth/Mars conversation and spacetime diagrams to explain that there is no universal collection of distant events that all observers must call “now.” He calls an intermediate region an “extended present” and concludes that “just now” does not exist. He cites Carlo Rovelli’s *Reality Is Not What It Seems* and *The Order of Time*. ## 12. Entropy and thermal time

Julius contrasts reversible-looking microscopic motion with macroscopic processes that dissipate energy: a ball striking the ground, a cooling drink, tire wear, stars aging, and tidal effects. His central claim, drawn from Rovelli, is that the observed arrow of time is associated with heat and coarse-graining. Thermal time reverses the intuitive order: time’s experienced flow emerges from our incomplete macroscopic description rather than acting as a universal external clock.
