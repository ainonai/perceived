## Institutions, incentives, and technology

A public position can bring rewards beyond agreement with its content. Company values can affect access to capital; a platform’s selections direct attention; an investment story can attract funding. To understand why a message spreads, ask what makes it useful to the person or organization advancing it. ### Information and public narratives

**ESG** stands for environmental, social, and governance criteria. A company may pursue a favorable score because it expects access to lending or institutional investment, independently of a manager’s personal beliefs. ### Social media and feedback

Your activity helps curate your feed. Lingering on one video and swiping past another give a recommendation system different signals. Resetting the account does not reset your interests. Julius expects familiar patterns to return through ordinary use, offering roughly 48 hours as his estimate for that return. [V201 · 01:40](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Vh7KSCqtCaI&t=100s) [V201 · 01:56](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Vh7KSCqtCaI&t=116s)

Cars and Muay Thai supply his personal examples. Different moods or times of day can initially produce different feeds, while similar viewing patterns draw them together. Borrow a friend’s phone and your own decisions about what to stop for would begin changing its recommendations too. [V201 · 01:56](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Vh7KSCqtCaI&t=116s) [V201 · 04:51](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Vh7KSCqtCaI&t=291s)

This is **heritability snowballing** in a personalized environment: existing interests guide attention, attention changes the feed, and the feed supplies more opportunities to pursue those interests. Self-analysis can compound in a similar way. Investing time and energy early changes what you notice and pursue later. ### AI, capability, and valuation

Julius’s 2026 AI argument separates a revolutionary technology from the claim that large language models already constitute **AGI**. Railroads and the internet provide the comparison. A technology can be useful on a vast scale while the financial story surrounding it promises more than current capability delivers. Pattern recognition, tool integration, agentic loops, general intelligence, and a profitable business are separate achievements. Faster customer-support replies can create real value without establishing general intelligence. Julius’s criticism targets the distance between that nearer capability and the larger promise used to attract funding. The investment question also extends beyond companies supplying AI infrastructure. Businesses using inexpensive AI might capture benefits without building it themselves. Finding such an opportunity and surviving until others recognize it are separate tasks: information can sit unread in a filing, while a contrary position can exhaust its capital before the wider market changes its mind.
