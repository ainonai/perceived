## The worldview in broad strokes

Julius proposes that inherited dispositions and evolutionary incentives explain much of attraction, behavior, success, and fulfillment. His dating account places physical attractiveness first, then interprets behavior as information about alternatives and underlying quality. Yet the fuller account distinguishes primal desire from practical attraction, initial selection from relationship maintenance, and numerical looks ratings from relative desirability. He acknowledges care, resources, personality, temperament, context, and the risk that insecurity itself drives a partner away. The same explanatory style extends outward: people partly select their environments; institutions respond to incentives; conscious choice has prior causes; useful action can coexist with acceptance of fate. ## The most important tensions to keep in view

1. **Appearance priority versus relational complexity.** His early personality-minor claim is broader than his later acknowledgment of care, fit, humor, and commitment.
2. **Naturalness versus management.** He criticizes imitation and outcome control while recommending concealed analysis, ambiguity, and planted rival cues.
3. **Genetic influence versus environmental efficacy.** Training, education, acting practice, and changing environments sit uneasily with claims that ordinary environment contributes nothing.
4. **Compassion versus hierarchy.** Unequal opportunities motivate kindness, but group rankings and reproductive-filtering speculation can undermine the dignity he also affirms.
5. **Skepticism versus selective certainty.** He identifies real research biases while sometimes using weaker anecdotes or favorite authorities as decisive evidence.
6. **Biological function versus personal or moral purpose.** A proposed explanation of why a trait evolved does not determine what a person should value or whether a treatment helps.

The chronology gives competing passages and dates rather than merely listing these as abstract objections.

## Provenance: three layers kept separate

**A — Julius’s expressed claims:** the main reconstruction, including unattractive, controversial, or unsupported claims. “He argues” and “he reports” do not certify truth.

**B — Editorial analysis:** relationships among his ideas, competing interpretations, and explicitly marked **inferred connections**. These do not supply access to anyone’s private motives.

**C — Independent evaluation:** external research, causal reasoning, methodological limits, and normative judgments identified as such. Scientific support for a narrow effect does not endorse every tactic or the larger worldview.
